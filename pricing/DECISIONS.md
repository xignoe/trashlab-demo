# DECISIONS.md: pricing surface

Every place a reasonable alternative existed. Newest at the bottom. Each entry: what was chosen, the alternative, why.

## Manager level (before Phase 1)

1. **types.ts rendered from the contract shorthand.** The contract says copy src/types.ts verbatim but ships a shorthand, not a TypeScript file. Chosen: render every entity with the exact field names, unions, and optional markers into exported types. Alternative: ask for the canonical file. Why: the merge step reconciles against the contract text, so exact field names are what matters, and stalling would cost a phase.

2. **Residential rates are zoned; frontload and rolloff are standard (no zone).** The contract prices cat_res_96 "in zone_open" and gives commercial prices without a zone. Chosen: residential RateVersions carry zoneId (zone_open at the contract prices, zone_boundary a few dollars higher), commercial RateVersions carry no zoneId. Why: it exercises both branches of resolvePrice precedence and gives the zones table something real to show. The boundary prices are invented and flagged as such in the seed.

3. **Prices the contract does not give.** cat_fl_2yd weekly $160 and 2x $280, cat_fl_3yd weekly $125, cat_res_96 boundary $31, cat_res_64 boundary $28, extra cart boundary $10, recycling boundary $13. Sourced from the research brief's small-hauler ranges. Alternative: leave those lines without a rate and let resolvePrice throw. Why: a ratebook with holes makes the increase and quote flows look broken.

4. **A prior superseded version for cat_res_96.** Added rv_res_96_open_weekly_2024 at $27 effective 2024-01-01, superseded by the 2025 version. Not in the contract. Why: the version history drawer and the "old version intact" scenario need history to exist before the first publish in the demo. It never wins resolvePrice for any date in 2025 or later.

5. **Blast radius "protected by contract" lists every contract account, not only in-scope ones.** The scenario asks a 4 percent residential increase to show acct_bakery (a frontload account) excluded. Chosen: the protected list includes every account with a Contract override, each with a note saying whether an override covers an item in the draft set or the account simply has no service in scope. Why: the owner's first fear is an increase reaching a contract account; the preview should say so by name every time, not only when the LOB happens to match.

6. **Cost to serve formula.** From the research brief (Waste360): truck day cost divided by lifts per day gives truck and labor per lift; disposal is yards times lb per yard by material times lifts, divided by 2000, times tip fee per ton; then 15 percent indirect. The brief adds 15 percent margin additively. Chosen: target price = full cost / (1 - margin) so margin is measured on price, which is how the persona talks about margin. Alternative: additive markup. Why: the difference is about 2 percent on a $90 quote and margin-on-price is what a P and L reports. Access flags add minutes per lift, which lowers effective lifts per day rather than adding a flat fee, so the cost effect is visible in the waterfall.

7. **Contract escalator is a single field.** Contract.escalator is one object, not a list, so "escalator entries" from the agent panel mean: write the escalator when the contract has none, and leave contracts that already have one alone (shown as already scheduled). Alternative: add an escalator history array. Why: the contract forbids type changes.

8. **Account level increases for non-contract accounts become draft RateVersions.** The model has no per-account price outside a Contract override, so approving a proposal for a non-contract account creates or joins a draft RateVersion for the rate that account resolves to, and the panel says how many accounts each draft covers. Alternative: write a Contract override at the higher price for each account. Why: that would turn a general increase into hundreds of contracts and contaminate the ratebook, which is the persona's stated failure mode.

9. **Quote exception measured against the ratebook, not the existing contract price.** For Sunrise Bakery the existing override makes resolvePrice return $198. The "X% below ratebook" line compares the quoted price with the published RateVersion price ($220), and the UI says so. Why: the persona wants exceptions measured against the standard price so they never disappear.

10. **Paper reachable through the plugin tool.** A raw HTTP probe of 127.0.0.1:29979 failed but get_basic_info succeeded (file "TrashLab, Haul-E Dispatch", two artboards, font Manrope). Tokens will be extracted from the real file in Phase 1.

11. **Project location.** No git worktrees exist in this checkout, so the surface is built as a standalone Vite app at /pricing (the repo path), serving routes /pricing and /pricing/quote. The merge step lifts src/ into the root app.

## Phase 1

12. **Scaffolded through a temp folder, React pinned to 18.** `npm create vite@latest` refuses a non-empty directory non-interactively, so the react-ts template was generated in the scratchpad and its files copied in (nothing existing was touched). The template's package.json now ships React 19, TypeScript 6, and Vite 8; the contract says React 18, so package.json was rewritten by hand: react 18.3, react-dom 18.3, @types/react 18, @vitejs/plugin-react 4, vite 6, typescript 5.8, tailwindcss 4 with @tailwindcss/vite, zustand 5, react-router-dom 6, vitest 3. Alternative: keep the template versions. Why: the contract names React 18, and the merge step is easier when every surface shares a major.

13. **Tailwind v4 via the Vite plugin, tokens mapped with `@theme inline`.** tokens.css holds `--tl-*` CSS variables; src/index.css maps them into Tailwind's theme namespaces (`--color-*`, `--font-*`, `--text-*`, `--radius-*`, `--shadow-*`) so utilities like `bg-surface` and `text-display` resolve to the token variables. Alternative: Tailwind v3 with tailwind.config.js reading `var(--tl-...)`. Why: v4 needs no PostCSS config and the inline theme keeps one source of truth in tokens.css.

Per the contract's fallback the token values are approximations of the Agent Console screenshot and DESIGN.md says so per token and per pattern. Alternative: stall until the limit resets. Why: the checklist rule is two failures then move on; later phases can re-extract when Paper is back and the mapping in DESIGN.md makes that a find-and-replace.

Alternative: hand-author JSON. Why: reproducibility and consistent totals. The JSON files are committed so nothing runs at build time.

Alternative: change the totals to what the engine would compute today. Why: the checklist values are required, and a posted invoice whose numbers were fixed under earlier rules is exactly what invariant 1 protects; Phase 2's invariant test only asserts the invoice is unchanged by a publish, not that it matches today's rules. This is logged so nobody "fixes" it later.

## Phase 2

30. **Engine state is an optional trailing parameter that defaults to the live store.** resolvePrice, computeCharge, and generateRecurringCharges take `(args, state = useStore.getState())` where state is an `EngineState` of the ten read-only tables. The contract argument objects are unchanged. Alternative: put `state?` inside the args object, or read the store globally. Why: the blast-radius preview must price "after" against a rateVersions array with drafts treated as published without touching the store; an explicit state makes the engine pure and the tests run on `cloneSeed()` with no reset dance, while UI callers keep the one-argument contract call.

31. **generateEventCharges, postInvoices, and allocate stay as throwing stubs.** The pricing surface never bills an event, posts an invoice, or touches a payment; the account tracker surface owns those bodies. The signatures are kept and the stubs say so in a comment so the merge sees six identical exports.

32. **Contract term is inclusive and a lapsed contract falls through to the ratebook.** resolvePrice only honours an override when onDate is within termStart to termEnd (date part, inclusive), per manager amendment 28. On 2026-08-31 contract_fl_004 still wins at 20900; on 2026-09-01 acct_fl_004 pays the standard 22000. Alternative: honour overrides forever. Why: the persona must see which contracts have run out.

33. **The last matching override in a contract wins.** saveContractOverride appends to an existing contract (see 36), so two overrides for the same catalog and frequency can coexist; resolvePrice prefers an exact frequency match and then the later row. Alternative: first match wins. Why: a renegotiated price saved today must take effect while the earlier row stays as history.

34. **RateVersion frequency is treated as "any" when absent.** A published version with no frequency matches every frequency but loses to an exact match with the same effectiveFrom. The seed has none; the rule is there so the contract's optional field means something. Ties on effectiveFrom then fall to the later publishedAt.

35. **Fee base allLines is computed like serviceLines.** computeCharge sees one line, so a percent fee with base allLines is taken on that line's baseCents and the code comment says so. Flat fees are cents (fee_env_1 value 100 is $1.00). Tax applies only when the zone's TaxRule lists the lineType; late fees skip tax before the rule is consulted; taxExempt accounts skip tax but keep fees. Every step rounds with Math.round. Worked example for the Phase 3 strip: base 2900, fuel 203, environmental 100, tax 217 on 3103, total 3420.

36. **saveContractOverride: replace only within a contract created today, otherwise append.** "Created today" means the contract id equals `contract_<accountId>_<yyyymmdd of today>`, since Contract has no createdAt. A brand new contract runs termStart today to termEnd exactly one year later (2026-09-10 to 2027-09-10, the checklist's "one year later" read literally) with renewalNoticeDays 60, and the account's contractId is set. On an older contract such as contract_bakery the new override is appended and the earlier one kept, so the workbench never silently rewrites a signed term; the term dates of an existing contract are not touched. Alternative: always replace the matching override. Why: an exception should stay visible and dated, which is the persona's "contain exceptions" rule.

37. **"Today" is pinned to 2026-09-10 in src/store/dates.ts.** The seed generator pins the same date (lapsed contract_fl_004, the 34 day old rolloff delivery, the 14 day event window). saveContractOverride defaults to it and accepts an explicit `today` for tests. Alternative: `new Date()`. Why: the demo stays coherent on any calendar day; the UI can pass a real date later without an engine change.

38. **generateRecurringCharges bills every cycle one period ahead, quarterly times three, and includes onCall items.** Period is cycleDate to the day before cycleDate plus cycleMonths (3 for quarterly, else 1). net30 and perJob accounts get one month; rolloff onCall items are billed as one haul per cycle so a rolloff increase has a visible blast radius (Hale Construction and Delgado move). Items are skipped before their effectiveFrom or after effectiveTo, with no proration (hauler policy proration none). An item with no published rate makes the run throw rather than silently drop the line. The rolloff surface owns real per-job billing.

39. **Blast radius evaluates on the later of onDate and the latest draft effectiveFrom, and reports that date.** Drafts default to the first of next month (Phase 4), so a preview run "today" would otherwise show zero movement. The result carries `evaluatedOn` so the modal can say "as of 2026-10-01". Monthly cents in movedAccounts are base service cents per month (resolved price times qty, no cycle multiplier, before fees and tax); totalMonthlyDeltaCents sums after minus before across moved accounts. Alternative: monthly totals including fuel and tax. Why: the owner asks what the rate change is worth; fees and tax scale with it and tax is not revenue.

40. **Blast radius scope rules.** Only billable accounts (active or pastDue) can move, so suspended acct_res_kerr and held-item acct_res_holt never appear; excludedContractAccounts lists every Contract row regardless of LOB (decision 5) with `protected` false and the note "contract ended <termEnd>, no longer protected" when the term has lapsed, "override covers <catalog names>" when an in-scope item is covered, "no service in this draft's scope, contract still protects it" when nothing is in scope, and, for honesty, "override does not cover <name>, that item moves" when a protected contract leaves an in-scope item uncovered (the account then also appears in movedAccounts). representativeAccounts carry `unchanged`, true when every line prices the same before and after, which is what the Phase 4 "Unchanged: contract override" label reads. A 4 percent residential increase moves 31 accounts and lists bakery and fl_001..004; a frontload increase moves acct_fl_004 (lapsed) but not fl_001..003 or bakery.

41. **Draft ids are readable and unique, in the seed's style.** createDraftRateVersion names a draft `rv_<catalog>_<zone or std>_<frequency>_<effectiveFrom yyyymmdd>` (rv_res_96_open_weekly_20261001) and appends `_2`, `_3` on collision. The id survives publish. Alternative: random ids. Why: the history drawer and runbook read better with ids that say what they are.

42. **Bulk increase rounds with (price * (100 + pct)) / 100.** `57500 * 1.025` is 58937.4999 in binary floating point and would round down; scaling by an integer-ish factor first keeps the half cent exact so a 2.5 percent increase on $575 gives $589.38. The bulk action picks the latest published version per (catalogId, zoneId, frequency) by effectiveFrom then publishedAt, so rv_res_96_open_weekly_2024 is never superseded twice. Running it twice creates a second set of drafts; the Phase 4 tray should discard before re-running.

43. **publishRateVersions ignores ids that are not pending drafts and returns other objects by identity.** The store maps the array and returns the same object for every version it does not publish; the invariant test checks the superseded rv_res_96_open_weekly is the same reference and deep equal to its snapshot. discardDraft removes only rows with status draft, so a published version can never be removed through it.

44. **Test files beyond the two the checklist names.** engine.test.ts and invariants.test.ts are as specified; preview.test.ts covers previewInvoice, blastRadius, and every Phase 2 store action so Phases 4 to 6 build on tested behaviour. 38 tests in 4 files.

## Phase 3

45. **Paper was reachable, so the Ratebook artboard is real (IA-0), not a local mock.** create_artboard succeeded on the first try; the 3.1 fallback (design/ratebook.html) was not needed and no such file exists. The artboard uses the tokens.css hex values inline because the Paper file still has no token set; DESIGN.md lists the group node ids. Alternative: also create a token set in the file with create_tokens. Why: the contract's token source of truth is tokens.css, and a second copy in Paper would drift.

46. **"Current" on a line is what resolvePrice returns, with a fallback to the latest published in the group.** src/lib/ratebook.ts calls resolvePrice({ catalogId, frequency, zoneId, onDate: TODAY }) with no account and accepts the result only if its rateVersionId is in the (catalogId, zoneId, frequency) group; otherwise (a zone line whose only published version is still in the future, so resolvePrice fell through to the standard rate) the line shows the latest published version with an amber "effective <date>" pill instead of the green rule pill. Alternative: pick the newest published version by date in the selector alone. Why: the table must show what a customer is billed today, and the only honest source for that is the engine.

47. **Read-side selectors live in src/lib/ratebook.ts and are tested.** catalogGroups, versionHistory, feeRuleSentence, and taxRuleSentence are pure over an EngineState so the table and the drawer cannot disagree about which version is current, and so the Phase 4 publish flow can assert on the same selectors. src/lib/ratebook.test.ts (5 tests) covers the res_96 group, a frontload standard-rate line with a synthetic draft, the two-version history chain with its "superseded by" back link, and the exact sentence strings from checklist 3.5. Alternative: compute inside the components. Why: the history drawer and the "old version intact" scenario in Phase 4 need a stable, testable definition of the version list.

48. **Worked example prices a synthetic account and site.** WorkedExample builds an EngineState from the live fee and tax rules plus one non exempt account at one zone_open site and calls computeCharge with baseCents 2900, so every number on the strip (including the "7% of $31.03" label, which sums base plus taxable fees off the returned Charge) comes from the engine. Alternative: use acct_res_001 and site_res_001 from the seed. Why: the strip should not change if someone later marks a seed account tax exempt or moves a site.

49. **Version history drawer sorts by effectiveFrom, then publishedAt, then id, newest first.** Drafts carry no publishedAt so a draft with the same effectiveFrom as a published version sorts after it; in practice drafts default to a future date and sit on top. "Superseded by" is derived by scanning for the version whose supersedesId points at the row, not stored, since RateVersion has no such field and types.ts cannot change. Both links highlight and scroll to the target card inside the same drawer.

50. **Phase 4 controls render disabled in their final slot.** The LOB tab row has an actions slot on the right holding "Increase all <LOB> by %" (outlined) and "New draft" (solid) as disabled buttons with a "Available in Phase 4" tooltip. Alternative: leave the slot empty. Why: the header should not change shape when Phase 4 lands, and the disabled state says honestly that nothing happens yet.

51. **setZonePublicPricing replaces the Zone row.** The only new store action this phase; it maps zones to a new array with the one row copied, so no seed object is mutated and reset() stays clean. Not served zones have the toggle disabled in the panel rather than guarded in the store, since the store action is generic and the rule is a UI one.

52. **Money formatting has one home, src/lib/money.ts.** formatCents renders integer cents as "$29.00" with thousands separators and a leading minus for negatives; formatPct renders 7 as "7%" and 2.5 as "2.5%". Every component in this phase imports these; no component divides by 100.

## Phase 4

58. **fee_env_1.base is "serviceLines" (C7).** Changed in src/seed/feeRules.json only; scripts/gen_seed.mjs does not write feeRules (it is one of the hand-authored small files). The Fee and tax rules panel sentence for a flat fee does not read base, so it still reads "Environmental fee: $1.00 flat per line, applies to recurring, not taxable". Posted seed charges keep the fees they were posted with.

59. **Flat fees apply once per whole month of the charge's period (C5 through C1).** computeCharge multiplies a flat fee by wholeMonthsIn(period), the number of whole calendar months in the inclusive period, with a floor of 1; a charge with servicedOn and no period counts as one month. Why: the rule is about the charge's period, and computeCharge is the one place every charge's fees are computed.

60. **publishedAt from the Ratebook is TODAY at 09:00 -04:00, one minute later per publish in a session (E1).** publishedAtFor(n) in src/pages/Ratebook.tsx replaces the wall clock. Alternative: a single fixed time for every publish. Why: the history drawer sorts same-day versions by publishedAt, so two publishes in one sitting must still order correctly without reading the clock.

61. **Scenario test for 4.10 runs through the store.** src/store/publishFlow.test.ts drives createBulkIncreaseDrafts, lobDrafts, blastRadius, publishRateVersions, and versionHistory exactly as the Ratebook does and asserts the checklist's numbers (31 moved, bakery and fl_001..003 protected with the out-of-scope note, fl_004 lapsed, history chain with the old version the same object, inv_res_maple_0001 at 8745).
