Hauler Billing Flow Map

A proposed high-level map of how money moves through a hauler's billing system, drawn across the three surfaces we are building: the pricing modeler defines what things cost, the account tracker turns services into invoices and payments, and the prospective customer buys off a public slice of the rate card. Every node below is tied to a real hauler policy, rate sheet, contract, or vendor help doc. Nothing in the flow is invented.

Evidence for each node

What real haulers and vendors do today at each step, with the source. Where practice varies by region or hauler size, the node says so.

1 · Pricing modeler

Owner or GM. Defines the catalog once so every quote and invoice is consistent.

P1
Service catalog by line of business

Residential is a cart (35/64/96 gal) at a frequency; frontload is a container size (1 to 8 yd) at pickups per week; rolloff is a box size with a rental period and a tonnage cap, or a haul fee plus disposal per ton. Franchise rate sheets publish the whole matrix, which is the clearest public picture of what a catalog contains.

WM SLO County rate sheet El Dorado Disposal rates Gleason rolloff pricing

P2
Zones carry more than price

TrashBolt's polygon system assigns products, pricing, taxes, delivery fee, fuel surcharge, route ID, service day, and billing cycle per polygon, and separates sales polygons (where you want customers) from operational polygons (where routes run) so an address on the wrong side of a street is rejected. Docket prices rolloff by zip and customer type; ServiceCore configures service areas with pricing tiers.

TrashBolt layered polygons Docket billing ServiceCore billing

P3
Rate card: zone × service × frequency

Howie's charges $90/quarter inside city limits and $126 outside; Xpress Dumpster varies rolloff price by county. WM SLO lists 2 yd frontload from $228.31 (1x/wk) to $1,025.76 (5x/wk). Haulers build the number from truck cost per lift plus disposal by density plus margin, which is why the same container is priced differently for a restaurant and an office.

Howie's Xpress Dumpster Waste360, Art of Commercial Pricing

P4
Fee and tax rules live on the line item

Casella's energy fee is a percent of invoice keyed to the DOE diesel index plus a fixed 9% environmental component; Republic charges an 18% environmental recovery fee and $5.95 per paper invoice. Washington's 3.6% solid waste tax applies to collection but not container rental or late fees. Jacksonville's 17% franchise fee audit found 6 of 10 haulers excluding admin, late, and fuel fees from the base. Small haulers often opt out entirely ("no fuel surcharges, no admin fees"). Varies widely by state and by hauler.

Casella fee table Republic fee disclosures WA DOR Jacksonville audit Home Town Disposal

P5
Contract overrides and escalators

Every commercial account is negotiated, so the real price is on the account, not the rate card. WM's commercial terms auto-renew, allow increases for disposal, CPI, and fuel, and treat other increases as accepted unless the customer objects within 30 days. County Waste's 24-month residential price lock steps up by 5% or CPI-U at term end. TrashLab's billing page already models "contract rates and price escalators" and schedulable annual escalations.

2 · Account tracker

Office manager or CSR. Sees customer, sites, services, and price in one place; runs the billing cycle.

A1
Account with one or more service locations

Docket, CurbWaste, and Hauler Hero all separate the billing entity from the service address. Docket assigns each client a type (Residential/Retail, Contractor Prepay, Commercial/Industrial, Contractor Bulk Bill) that drives prepay vs net terms and per-job vs bulk invoicing. Parent/child accounts are a known gap: Hauler Hero reviewers call them a work in progress.

Docket billing workflow guide Hauler Hero reviews

A2
Agreement of priced service items

Service = container or cart + frequency + material + rate, where the rate resolves from the contract if present, else the rate card for the zone. Hauler Hero groups accounts into billing "payment groups"; TrashLab lists billable events as completed jobs, route events, container movement, and disposal data.

Hauler Hero billing TrashLab billing features

B1
Service change: policy decides proration

Evergreen charges $40 for a cart size swap; Saint Paul charges $40 if you change cart size more than once a quarter; Preferred Waste and GFL do not prorate at all; County Waste prorates first and last partial months. The product needs an effective date on the change and a per-hauler policy for what happens to the current cycle. Varies by hauler.

Evergreen Saint Paul Preferred Waste GFL FAQ

A3
Recurring charges in advance, by cycle

Residential is mostly quarterly or monthly in advance (Home Town $87.45/quarter, Howie's first quarter paid at signup, WM residential "billed in advance" monthly, quarterly, or annually). Frontload is flat monthly. Rolloff retail is charged at booking; contractor charges accumulate in Docket's Charge Bank until the monthly bulk invoice.

Home Town Disposal WM residential terms Docket Charge Bank

B2
Field events feed billing

Drivers log overloads and extra bags with photos in the Hauler Hero app so the office can follow up or escalate (the article stops short of auto-charging). Scale tickets return net tons; Gleason charges the card on file for overage, Docket reads tickets with Weight Ticket AI. Routeware names "the extra pickup that never gets charged" as the core leakage.

Hauler Hero on overloads Gleason Routeware

A4
Event charges: extras, tonnage, dry runs

Preferred bills $2.50 per loose bag and $25 per unscheduled return trip; Xpress charges $150 if a box is blocked; rolloff overage runs $70 to $100 per ton after the cap; extra rental days $15 to $50. TrashLab lists exactly this set as "exception billing": overages, extra rental days, contamination, relocation, failed service.

Preferred Waste Xpress Dumpster TrashLab billing

A5
Review queue with proof of service

TrashLab's stated workflow: audit completed work, exceptions, proof of service, and customer rules before invoices are sent, with photos and disposal tickets attached so disputes resolve against evidence. CurbWaste's billing docs have a "Needs Action" exception tab for the same purpose.

TrashLab billing features CurbWaste billing KB

A6
Invoice delivered by mail, email, or portal

Paper is still common enough that Republic charges $5.95 per paper invoice and WM waives its admin charge only for paperless plus autopay. Small haulers still publish PO boxes for checks.

Republic fees WM residential terms

A7
Payment and allocation

Check, card by phone, card and ACH online, autopay runs, cash at the scale, and (Docket) the driver taking payment on site. Card usually carries a fee ($1.99 at WM, 3% at Preferred) with ACH free; autopay is discounted ($1/mo at Howie's) or mandatory (County Waste). Partial payments and credits need allocation across invoices, which the ERD already models as PAYMENT_ALLOCATION.

Docket payments Howie's Preferred Waste

A8
Accounting sync and lump-sum deposits

Processors deposit many customer payments as one lump sum, so staff group payments into Undeposited Funds, add fees as negative lines, and match the bank feed or revenue gets duplicated. QuickBooks sync is the single most common billing complaint across vendor reviews (one-way sync, parent-account dumping, QBO-only).

Docket QBO reconciliation ServiceCore reviews Trash Flow vs QuickBooks

A9
Dunning: late fee, suspend, reinstate, remove

Preferred: $10 late fee at day 5, two-pickup grace, then suspension and a reinstatement fee plus one month in advance. Home Town: suspended accounts must pay 24 hours before pickup day or lose the week. Bill's: $7 late fee, $5 reinstatement. Franchise areas: 1.5%/month finance charge, $25 reinstatement, $75 cart pickup. City-billed systems roll unpaid balances onto the property tax bill.

3 · Prospective customer

New homeowner or small business. Wants a price for their address and a start date without calling.

C1
Address first, then everything else

Waste Connections: "tell us where you live, we'll check availability and show you the price." WM's location pages hide price behind "Find Availability." TrashBolt reports that once a hauler has online signup, about half of new signups arrive through it and 30% of those after hours. Most platforms, TrashLab included, bolt this on rather than build it natively.

Waste Connections WM location page Waste360 on TrashBolt

C2
The public price is a slice, with its fees

Trash Taxi of Georgia shows the fee stack up front: $27.95 setup, $1/mo EPA fee, 7% fuel surcharge, monthly rate billed quarterly. Dresnek sells "Waste Pickup, Quarterly Billing, $72" as a WooCommerce product. Commercial and contractor pricing stays off the page because it is negotiated per account.

Trash Taxi signup Dresnek

C3
Choose cart, add-ons, start date

WM's online subscription flow picks a start date and promises setup "in a few minutes" with no annual contract. Red Oak Sanitation asks for the start date and payment up front. Cart delivery happens before the first pickup.

WM moving page Red Oak Sanitation

C4
Pay the first cycle in advance

Howie's collects the first quarter at signup; WM bills in advance; Texas Pride requires a 3-month non-refundable commitment; County Waste requires autopay. Rolloff retail is charged at booking with the card kept on file for overage (Gleason, Budget Dumpster). This payment is what creates the account and the delivery work order upstream.

Howie's Texas Pride Budget Dumpster terms

C5
Portal: pay, autopay, request, hold

Republic's portal is the fullest public feature list: pay with saved methods, one-click autopay, paperless, schedule extra or bulk pickup, report a missed pickup, relocate a container, seasonal stop and restart, multi-site switching. GFL has a one-button vacation hold. TrashLab's portal turns service requests into dispatchable work and states that portal payments should "integrate with the billing system rather than existing as isolated transactions," which is why C5 feeds A7 and B1 rather than a side ledger.

What the ERD has, and what the billing flow needs added

Your entity map already carries the account and operations spine. The billing flow above needs a pricing layer and a charge layer that are not yet on it. Highlighted rows are the additions.

Where the three surfaces pull against each other

Price by street vs price you can publish. The pricing modeler wants zone-level and contract-level pricing because route density sets the real cost (about $8 per lift at 80 lifts a day, per Waste360). The storefront needs one number a homeowner will trust. The resolution in the map is that C2 shows only the public subset of P3 for the matched zone, and commercial stays behind a quote request.

Effective-dated changes vs "we don't prorate." The account tracker needs effective dates on every service item to make B1 clean. Many haulers have written policies against proration and will keep them. The product should treat proration as a per-hauler policy on the billing cycle, not a default behavior.

Exceptions that should be billed vs relationships the office protects. B2 produces more billable events than A4 will ever invoice, on purpose. The review queue (A5) is where that judgment happens, which argues for making "waive" a first-class action with a reason, so leakage is measured rather than invisible.
