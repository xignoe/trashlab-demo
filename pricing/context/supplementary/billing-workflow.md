# Billing and Payments Workflow, End to End

## Operating reality in a small hauler's office

A two-to-four-person office rarely has separated departments. One person answers calls and dispatches; another handles billing and deposits; an owner approves credits and chases large balances. Work moves through calls, email, driver notes, route software, payment portals, spreadsheets, and accounting software, often with duplicate entry.

## 1. Service changes become billable records

Changes begin with a call, email, online request, salesperson, or driver observation: start, stop, pause; change size, quantity, frequency; extra pickup, swap, relocation, bulky item; contamination, overflow, blocked access, excessive weight; change price, billing address, tax status, PO, payment method.

The CSR verifies the account, quotes any charge, chooses an effective date, and creates a service order or edits the recurring record. Dispatch adds the work to a route. Deliveries and removals should update asset inventory.

Manual: interpreting unusual requests, calculating prorations, checking contract pricing, entering emailed changes, ensuring dispatch and billing both received the update. Drivers report exceptions on paper, by text, or verbally.

Leakage: work performed without a charge; canceled service still billing; price increases missing accounts; container inventory and billing disagreeing; wrong effective dates creating overcharge and credit.

## 2. Billing run preparation

Residential billed monthly or quarterly in advance. Commercial monthly recurring plus events. Rolloff combines haul, rental days, tickets, tonnage, fees, taxes.

Before running: new starts, stops, mid-cycle changes; completed extras, swaps, emergencies; driver exceptions with notes and photos; scale tickets and weights; contract rates, minimums, surcharges; prior credits, deposits, unapplied payments. The clerk reviews an exception report or scans totals for anything that "looks wrong." Deadline pressure causes undocumented items to be deferred and forgotten.

## 3. Invoice generation and delivery

Posted to AR and delivered by email, portal, print-and-mail, or EDI. Fallout: missing invoices, wrong contacts, PO requirements, portal uploads, tax exemption questions, disputes. An invoice can appear "sent" while in spam or waiting on a procurement portal upload.

## 4. Payments

Autopay: stored card or ACH on due date; staff handle declines, returns, expired cards; disputes when invoices contain unexpected extras.
ACH: cheap, but transfers often lack invoice references; returns reopen invoices days later.
Cards: processor settles a net deposit after fees bundling many customers; staff manage expired cards, declines, refunds, chargebacks.
Checks: open mail, identify customer and invoice from memo, post, deposit; combined payments across locations require investigation.

A payment is complete only when it settles in the bank and is applied to the correct receivable.

## 5. Collections, late fees, suspension

Aging review several times a week: resend, statements, calls, promises to pay, disputes. Small balances sit for months because calling costs more than the balance. Late fees at a threshold, often waived for good customers or documented delivery failures. Suspension list reviewed by owner; dispatch marks accounts so drivers skip them. Payment triggers reinstatement, sometimes with a restart fee. Poor sync between payments, billing, and dispatch makes this fragile.

## 6. Credits and adjustments

From missed pickups, duplicates, wrong rates, cancellations, returned containers, settlements. CSR documents reason; manager approves larger amounts; applied against an invoice or left on account. Weak controls: legitimate credits take too long, or staff use credits as corrections without fixing the underlying record, so the error repeats.

## 7. Reconciliation

Card and ACH batches vs processor deposits; checks vs bank deposits; fees, refunds, chargebacks, returns; billing receipts vs accounting; bank vs GL. Month-end: bank, AR aging, unapplied cash, credit balances, clearing accounts, processor receivables. Differences researched through spreadsheets and exports.

## Five most painful problems, ranked

1. Field activity does not reliably become a billable charge. Driver judgment, paper tickets, incomplete notes resist automation.
2. Customer, service, and pricing data drift across systems. Different data models and integrations leave gaps and duplicate entry.
3. Collections consume disproportionate time. Delinquency is caused by disputes, missing POs, ownership changes, cash flow, not forgotten invoices.
4. Payments are hard to match and reconcile. Batches, net fees, later reversals, inconsistent remittance data.
5. Credits, late fees, suspensions require judgment. Rigid automation either angers good customers or fails to enforce policy.
