entry that changes an earlier choice says so, and the earlier entry points forward to it.

1. Event rate table location. Added `src/seed/eventRates.json` with extraBags 250, overload 1000, contamination 2000, dryRun 2500 (cents), typed locally in engine.ts. Alternative was adding an EventRate entity to types.ts. The contract forbids editing types.ts, and the brief asks for a small table in seed, so the table is seed only and the merge can lift it into a type later.

2. Event charges are flat per event. An extraBags event bills one line at the table rate regardless of bag count in the driver note. Alternative: parse a count from the note and multiply. Notes are free text and the persona doc warns against charges that cannot be explained; a flat per-event line with the note attached as evidence is explainable and disputable.

3. Cycle cadence rules. monthly and net30 accounts are due on the first of every month; quarterly on Jan 1, Apr 1, Jul 1, Oct 1; perJob never generates recurring charges. Alternative: anniversary cycles per account. Calendar cycles match "quarterly in advance" practice in the brief and make the batch a single run.

4. Accounts on hold still bill. acct_res_holt (vacation hold) generates recurring charges because Piedmont's proration policy is none and holds do not stop billing under that policy. Alternative: skip hold accounts like suspended ones. Only suspended accounts are excluded, matching invariant 4 exactly.

5. Prior-cycle comparison is computed, not stored. To flag "changed from prior cycle" the store runs generateRecurringCharges for the prior cycle date on a throwaway copy of the db and diffs by serviceItemId, totalCents, and rateVersionId. Alternative: compare against posted invoices from the prior cycle. Computing keeps the comparison consistent with the engine and works for accounts with no seeded history.

6. Idempotent generation. A serviceItem and period start that already has an approved or posted Charge is skipped on re-run; a ServiceEvent or ScaleTicket that already has a Charge is skipped. Alternative: always generate and dedupe at posting. Skipping at generation keeps the queue honest.

7. Due dates. net30 invoices are due 30 days after issue; monthly and quarterly invoices are due 15 days after issue. The hauler late fee day (5) counts from the due date. Alternative: due on receipt. Kept simple and visible on the invoice.

8. Invoice grouping. postInvoices creates one invoice per account from the given charges, so Oakridge's four sites land on one invoice with per-site lines. Alternative: one invoice per site. The contract seed says Oakridge is "one invoice".

9. Edit amount audit. Editing a proposed amount keeps the original proposed base visible: the store keeps an `edits` sidecar (chargeId, originalBaseCents, newBaseCents, reason, by, at) rather than adding fields to Charge. Alternative: mutate description only. Sidecar keeps types.ts untouched and the audit queryable.

10. Unapplied cash definition. A payment's unapplied amount is its cents minus the sum of its allocations. The bucket lists every payment with a positive remainder, whether or not an account is known. Alternative: a separate "unmatched" status on Payment. The contract Payment status has no such value, so the remainder is derived.

11. acct_pm_oakridge is taxExempt in seed. The contract does not say which account is tax exempt; a property manager is the most plausible exempt payer and it gives the taxExempt test a real account.

12. Invoice numbers. `INV-2026-0001` style, sequential across the hauler, continuing from the highest seeded number. Alternative: per-account sequences. Hauler-wide sequences match what QuickBooks expects.

13. Historical exception events live in serviceEvents.json. The six waived charges and the one posted contamination line (acct_fl_004, August) point at seven real ServiceEvent rows dated before the 14 day window (ids prefixed `evt_hist_`), each already carrying a Charge. Alternative: dangling source ids. Real rows keep evidence links resolvable; generateEventCharges must skip events that already have a Charge (decision 6), so the five in-window exceptions remain the only new event charges.

14. Vacation hold is account state, not service item state. acct_res_holt has status hold and a Request req_holt_vacation; its ServiceItem stays active so decision 4 (hold accounts still bill under proration none) holds in the engine without a special case.

15. Batch composition. batch_0908 pays 14 distinct invoices: five monthly 96 gal accounts, two monthly 96 gal plus recycling, one quarterly 96 gal (pay_card_014, overpaid by 1530), three quarterly 64 gal, one quarterly 64 gal plus recycling, and two contract frontload accounts. Two invoices carry an extra posted line (a delivery fee on acct_res_002, whose service started 2026-08-20, and an August contamination on acct_fl_004) because no set of pure recurring totals under the seed fee and tax rules sums to 131842 minus 1530. Alternative: bend the fee math or the gross. The gross and fee figures are contract values, so the invoices bent instead, and both extra lines are realistic.

16. Every rolloff ticket has a work order. ticket_hale_3 (under cap) needed a parent, so wo_hale_haul_3 exists alongside the two hauls named in the contract. Hauls are kind dumpAndReturn. The homeowner prepaid box is a posted invoice (haul plus delivery fee) paid by card on 2026-08-07 so the account has coherent history and zero unapplied cash.

17. Seed invoice numbers start at INV-2026-0201 and run chronologically by issue date, so decision 12 (continue from the highest seeded number) starts Phase 3 posting at INV-2026-0223.

18. Tokens came from the Paper file: the manager read the "Account view - Office" artboard (D3-0) and the dispatch nav (2-0) with the Paper MCP and recorded the values in docs/paper-reference.md because the subagent MCP quota was exhausted, and tokens.css copies those values exactly.

19. Engine db context is a binding, not an import. `src/store/db.ts` exports `setEngineDb(db | () => db)`, `getEngineDb()`, and `withEngineDb(db, fn)`; `Db` is the `Seed` type. Tests bind a `loadSeed()` clone, the store binds a getter to its live state, and the prior-cycle comparison (decision 5) runs inside `withEngineDb` on a throwaway clone. Alternative: pass db as a parameter to every engine function. The contract fixes the signatures without a db argument, and a getter binding keeps them pure while still reading live state. `getEngineDb()` throws when nothing is bound so a forgotten binding fails loudly instead of pricing against an empty db.

20. Contract overrides apply only inside the contract term. resolvePrice uses an override when termStart <= onDate <= termEnd; outside the term the rate card applies. Alternative: honor an override whenever the contract row exists. An expired contract that keeps discounting is exactly the leakage this surface exists to catch, and the bakery escalator anniversary (2027-01-01) falls after termEnd (2026-12-31), so it is a renewal question for a person, not a pricing rule for the engine.

21. Charge ids. computeCharge assigns `chg_` plus a zero padded counter (`chg_0001`), skipping any id already in the db, through an injectable generator (`setChargeIdGenerator`, `resetChargeIds`); an explicit `id` in the input is honored. Alternative: a random suffix. A counter is deterministic under tests and readable in the queue; the skip check keeps it safe next to the named seed ids.

22. Frequency matching in pricing. A RateVersion or contract override with no frequency is a wildcard; one with a frequency must equal the requested frequency. When computeCharge is given a catalogId and no pricing, it resolves with the site zone, the account, the period start or servicedOn, and the frequency passed by the caller or, failing that, the site's ServiceItem for that catalog. Charges with no catalog default to ruleWon standardRate, or manualException when the source is manual, so invariant 2 holds for fee and late fee lines too. Alternative: require frequency on every computeCharge call. Event and fee lines have no natural frequency, so the inference keeps the contract signature usable for every line type.

23. Paper artboards were drafted by the manager, not by phase subagents. Subagents received "Weekly MCP limit reached" from the Paper MCP while the manager session could still call it, and the shared Paper file changed under the build (the dispatch console artboards were gone by Phase 1 and sibling surfaces had added "Account view - Office" and "Pricing, Ratebook"). The manager read the account view's computed styles into docs/paper-reference.md, created "Billing run, Office" (P2-0) and "Billing payments, Office" (XV-0) next to the sibling artboards, and described both layouts in the same file. Alternative: skip Paper for this surface. The contract asks for a Paper artboard per screen before code, so the manager kept the step and moved it to the only session that could perform it.

23b. Posting commits through applyPosting. (Numbered 23b because two entries were both written as 23; the first 23 is the Paper entry above.) postInvoices({ chargeIds }) stays pure like the rest of the engine: it validates (every id exists, is approved, and sits on no locked invoice), groups by account, and returns new Invoice rows without touching the db. The caller commits with `applyPosting(db, invoices)`, which returns a new Db with the invoices appended and every named charge marked posted. The same pattern covers the other writers: `appendCharges(db, charges)` for generation results and `applyWaive(db, waiveCharge(input))` in src/store/waive.ts. Alternative: have postInvoices flip charge status on the rows it was given. A pure function plus an explicit apply keeps the prior-cycle comparison (entry 5) and tests free of hidden writes, and gives the zustand store one `set(state => ({ db: applyX(state.db, rows) }))` shape for every action.

24. Rates are monthly and onCall items never recur. RateVersion priceCents is a monthly price, so generateRecurringCharges bills price x qty x months in the period (a quarterly 96 gal cart is 3 x 2900 = 8700, which is what the seed history and batch_0908 invoices already show). ServiceItems with frequency onCall (the rolloff boxes on acct_contractor_hale and acct_ro_homeowner) are billed per haul from a WorkOrder, never as a recurring line, so they are skipped even when the account is due. Alternative: bill the quarterly account one month of price per quarter, or bill an onCall item as a monthly rental. Both contradict the seeded invoices, and the haul line for the homeowner box in the seed is an event line, not a recurring one.

25. Overage lines price as standardRate with no rate version, and the engine has a fixed clock. The rolloff overage rate lives on the catalog (overageCentsPerTon), not on a RateVersion, so an overage Charge carries pricing { ruleWon: 'standardRate' } and catalogId cat_ro_20yd so the panel can show the formula and cap. Event lines from the eventRates table carry the same ruleWon. postInvoices uses `today()` from engine.ts (2026-09-10, the demo date) for issuedAt, with `setToday()` for tests and for a store control that moves the clock. Alternative: a ruleWon value for catalog formulas, or issuedAt equal to the cycle date. types.ts fixes the ruleWon union, and the checklist fixes issuedAt to today.

26. Addendum C12 now governs runtime ids and supersedes entry 21. From Phase 4 billing mints `chg_bl_####` charge ids, `inv_bl_####` invoice ids, and `rv_bl_####` ids for the RateVersion stub, with counters above the highest existing suffix. Entry 21 stays as the record of the Phase 2 choice.

27. Addendum C5 now governs flat fees on multi-month charges. Through Phase 3 billing applied fee_env_1 once per charge, so acct_res_maple's quarterly 96 gal line was 10061 in the engine and in the seed history (chg_maple_2026q3_1). From Phase 4 a flat fee applies once per whole month in the charge's period, which gives 10261. No earlier entry recorded the once-per-charge behavior; this entry records the change.

28. Addendum E1 governs where the engine clock lives, refining entry 25. TODAY stays inside engine.ts until polish, then moves to src/store/clock.ts in Phase 7.

29. Addendum C5 seed fallout: one batch payment short pays. With env 300 on every quarterly line, the fourteen invoices paid in batch_0908 total 1200 cents more than before (five quarterly invoices, one with a recycling line), so full payment plus the 1530 overpay would exceed the 131842 gross. The targets are contract values, so scripts/gen_seed.ts bends one payment: pay_card_011 short pays inv_res_020_2026q3 (acct_res_020) by exactly the computed shortfall, 1200 cents, and allocates only what it paid, which leaves 1200 open on that invoice. The generator computes the shortfall rather than hard coding it and throws if it goes negative. Every other target still holds (gross 131842, fees 4120, net 127722, 14 payments, pay_card_014 unapplied 1530, Maple open 8745, Oakridge check covering its three invoices in full) and the generator now also asserts env equals 100 per whole month on every historical recurring line. Alternative: spread small short pays across the quarterly accounts, or swap a quarterly invoice out of the batch. One short pay is the smallest change and is easy to explain on the Payments tab.

30. Whole months for a flat fee. wholeMonths(period) in src/store/cycles.ts counts calendar months whose first and last day both fall inside the period and floors at 1, so a charge with any period takes a flat fee at least once. Every period the engine generates is whole months, so the floor only matters for hand built or future mid month periods. Alternative: prorate a flat fee by days. Piedmont's policy is proration none, and a fee that silently drops to zero on a partial period would be leakage.

31. The billing store (src/store/useStore.ts) keeps src/store/state.ts as its data shape. An edit is a decision: editAmount re-prices the charge through computeCharge with the same id, sets pricing.ruleWon to manualException (addendum C11) while keeping rateVersionId or contractId for lineage, marks the charge approved, and appends a row to the `edits` sidecar (entry 9). Every edit appends; originalBaseCents always carries the first proposed base, so repeated edits never lose it. Alternative: leave an edited charge proposed and require a second approve. The inline form already shows before and after totals and asks for confirmation, so a second click adds friction without adding control.

32. Re-running a cycle replaces its proposed set and keeps ids. runCycle drops the run's still-proposed rows, regenerates against the db without them, and gives each regenerated row the id of the row it replaces (matched by source type, source id, and period start or servicedOn). Approved, waived, and posted rows stay; the engine already skips them (entry 6). The prior-cycle diff (entry 5) runs generateRecurringCharges per account for that account's prior cycle date (Sep 1 for monthly, Jul 1 for quarterly on the Oct 1 run) on a clone with an empty charges table and throwaway `chg_bl_prior_` ids, so it neither skips on posted history nor consumes the runtime counter's ids in the db. A recurring charge is flagged serviceChange when its service item is absent from the prior run, rateChange when its rate version or contract differs, and amountChange when only the total differs. Alternative: no-op on a re-run. Replacing lets a rate published between runs show up, which the Phase 5 scenario needs.

33. What the queue holds. queueItems lists every event charge in the current run (exception events and overages, in any status) plus every recurring charge in changedFromPrior, with undecided rows first. Decided rows stay visible with their status pill, and the "All decisions made. Ready to post." state appears above them once nothing is undecided. bulkApproveClean approves only proposed charges that are not queue items. Alternative: drop decided rows from the queue. Keeping them lets a person see what was waived or edited in the same cycle before posting.

34. Deciding an item moves to the next one. When the selected queue item is approved, edited, or waived, selection moves to the next undecided item below it in queue order, wrapping to the top; when nothing undecided is left, the decided item stays selected so the person sees what they did. Deciding a row that is not selected leaves the selection alone. The next item is read from the queue before the decision lands (nextUndecidedAfter in src/store/selectors.ts), because the queue re-sorts decided rows to the bottom. Alternative: jump to the first undecided row. That skips past the rows a person was working down.

35. RateVersion publishing is a local stub (shared/OWNERSHIP.md: pricing owns publishing a RateVersion). publishRateVersionStub() in src/store/stubs.ts appends a published RateVersion with an `rv_bl_####` id (addendum C12), publishedAt on the engine day, the frequency of the version it supersedes, and supersedesId set to the latest published version for the same catalog and zone effective on or before the new date. The "Rates" drawer on /billing lists the published versions for cat_res_96 only, marks the one each zone resolves to on the cycle date, and its form (price in dollars stored as priceCents, effectiveFrom, zoneId) calls the stub. The drawer says in plain words that Pricing publishes rates and this control stands in until merge. At merge the drawer goes away and billing reads Pricing's RateVersions; nothing else changes because billing already resolves prices through resolvePrice on the period start. Alternative: seed a second 96 gal version effective Nov 1. That would bake the scenario into seed and hide the moment a price changes.

36. Leakage window. A cycle dated D owns the waives whose WaivedCharge.at falls in the month before D ([prior month start, D)), because the D run is worked, decided, and posted in that month: the Oct 1 cycle owns waives recorded Sep 1 through Sep 30. The leakage card sums the current cycle and the two before it, so on the Oct 1 cycle it counts waives recorded Jul 1 through Sep 30 (four seeded rows; the two April rows fall outside), and breaks the same set down by reason, by route (charge.siteId to site.routeId, "No route" when the site has none), and by account. Dollars are the waived charges' totals, so the card agrees with the dollars at issue a person gave up. Alternative: attribute a waive to the cycle of the charge's service date or period. WaivedCharge.at is the only field every waive carries, and it is when the money was given up. With the engine clock fixed at 2026-09-10, waives recorded after advancing to Nov 1 still carry Sep 10, so they count toward the Oct 1 cycle; they stay inside the three cycle window through the Dec 1 cycle. Addendum E1 revisits the clock: entry 41 moves the clock with Next cycle, so a waive in the Nov 1 run carries Oct 10 and counts toward the Nov 1 cycle.

37. What posting takes. Post invoices is enabled only when no queue item is undecided and at least one approved charge in the run is waiting. It posts every approved charge in the run, one invoice per account. Clean charges that nobody approved stay proposed and off the invoices; the confirmation says how many and suggests bulk approve first, rather than approving them silently. Once every charge in the run is posted or waived, the first two tiles switch to Invoices posted and Posted total, the dark card becomes "Posted", the header offers "Next cycle", and a "Posted this cycle" list shows number, account, total, due date, and a lock per invoice with its lines one click away. Earlier posted cycles stay reachable from that card, which is how the October invoice is checked after advancing. Alternative: approve all clean lines as part of posting. Posting is irreversible, so it should never widen what a person approved.

38. The edit sidecar also records originalTotalCents (extends entry 9), so an edited item shows the first proposed total struck through next to the new one in the queue row and the detail panel, not only the original base.

39. Next cycle moves the cycle date only. advanceCycle() sets cycleDate to the next month start and clears the selection; the engine clock stays on 2026-09-10, so invoices posted from a later cycle carry issuedAt 2026-09-10. The control appears only after the current run is fully posted. Alternative: move the engine clock with the cycle (setToday). That changes tenure, due dates, and suggestion inputs mid demo; addendum E1 moves the clock to src/store/clock.ts in Phase 7 and is the place to revisit it. Superseded by entry 41: Next cycle now also moves the clock one month.

40. Applying unapplied cash is a local stub (shared/OWNERSHIP.md: account owns taking a payment and allocating it). applyUnappliedStub() in src/store/stubs.ts takes a payment id and a list of invoice ids with cents, checks that each invoice is posted and belongs to the payer's account, and calls the engine's allocate() bound to the local store; the store action applyUnapplied commits the returned rows to db.allocations. Over-allocation (more than the payment's unapplied cents, or more than an invoice's open balance) is refused by allocate() and its message is shown inline on the Payments tab as written. The Apply form lists only the payer's posted invoices with an open balance, oldest first, prefilled oldest first up to the unapplied cash; when none are open it offers only Leave on account. Leave on account records the choice in UI state (leftOnAccount) and changes no balance. Unapplied cash is a payment's cents minus its allocations (entry 10), so a short pay such as pay_card_011 (entry 29) is an open balance on inv_res_020_2026q3, not unapplied cash; the batch table marks it as short on its row and the batch callout explains it. A short pay is counted only on invoices issued on or before the payment's receivedAt, so pay_card_014's 1530 applied to its October invoice (issued after the payment arrived) leaves that invoice open without being called short. The Payments tab tiles use a seven day window ending on the engine day (Sep 4 to Sep 10): received and applied count payments received in that window, unapplied counts every payment on record, and processor fees count batches deposited in that window. At merge the Apply control goes away and Account's action writes the same PaymentAllocation rows. Alternative: auto-apply unapplied cash to the next posted invoice. That moves money without a person, which invariant 7 rules out.

41. Addendum E1: the clock lives in src/store/clock.ts, and Next cycle moves it one month. clock.ts exports TODAY = '2026-09-10', today(), setToday(), and stamp(), which returns noon Eastern daylight time on the engine day (`2026-09-10T12:00:00-04:00`). engine.ts re-exports today() and setToday() from it. Every runtime timestamp comes from stamp(): WaivedCharge.at, the edit sidecar's at, a run's ranAt, the RateVersion stub's publishedAt, Leave on account, and Invoice.postedAt (issuedAt and dueAt stay calendar dates, like the seed). No file in src/store outside clock.ts and tests constructs a Date; cycles.ts does its calendar math on day numbers, and clock.test.ts scans for it. Next cycle moves the cycle date to the next month start and the clock to the same day of the next month (2026-09-10 to 2026-10-10, clamped at month end), and reset() returns it to TODAY. So each run is worked in the month before its cycle date: the November run's invoices are issued 2026-10-10 and due 2026-10-25 (net30 2026-11-09), and a waive in the November run carries 2026-10-10, which entry 36's window gives to the Nov 1 cycle. Alternative: keep the clock on 2026-09-10 (entry 39), or set it to the cycle date itself. A fixed clock issues November invoices before October's are due and books November waives to the October cycle, so the leakage card's current cycle never moves after advancing. The cycle date itself would book every waive to the following cycle under entry 36, since a cycle owns the month before its date. One month forward keeps the October relationship (worked 21 days before the cycle date) for every later cycle. What moves with it: the Payments tiles window becomes Oct 4 to Oct 10, so it reads zero received after advancing, because the seed has no October payments; tenure grows by one month, which changes no suggestion in the seed.

42. Late fees are not generated by the billing run. computeCharge prices a lateFee line (no fees, never taxed), but runCycle generates recurring and event lines only, so acct_res_maple's past due Q3 balance does not pick up the hauler's $10 fee. Alternative: have the run add a lateFee line for every posted invoice more than lateFeeDay days past due on the engine day. The contract gives generateRecurringCharges and generateEventCharges only, the checklist names no late fee job, and adding one to the run would put a charge in front of a person that no queue lane or suggestion rule explains. A late fee job is a later addition that would use the existing lateFee line type.
